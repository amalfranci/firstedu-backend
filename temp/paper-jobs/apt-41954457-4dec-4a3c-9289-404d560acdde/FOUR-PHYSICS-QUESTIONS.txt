A particle of mass $m$ and charge $q > 0$ is projected from the origin $(0,0,0)$ at $t=0$ with an initial velocity $\vec{v}_0 = 3 v_0 \hat{i}$ in a region containing a uniform electric field $\vec{E} = E_0 \hat{j}$ and a uniform magnetic field $\vec{B} = B_0 \hat{k}$, where $v_0 = \frac{E_0}{B_0}$. The trajectory of the particle lies entirely in the $xy$-plane. The radius of curvature of the particle's path at the point where its $y$-coordinate reaches its minimum value is:

A) $\frac{m E_0}{2 q B_0^2}$
B) $\frac{m E_0}{q B_0^2}$
C) $\frac{2 m E_0}{q B_0^2}$
D) $\frac{9 m E_0}{2 q B_0^2}$

Correct: A

Explanation:
**Insight:** The trajectory of a charged particle in crossed electric and magnetic fields can be analyzed by solving the equations of motion. By finding the velocity and acceleration at the point where the $y$-coordinate is minimum, we can determine the radius of curvature using the formula $R = \frac{v^2}{a_n}$, where $a_n$ is the component of acceleration perpendicular to the velocity.

To find the radius of curvature at the minimum $y$-coordinate, we first write the equations of motion for the particle of mass $m$ and charge $q$ in the given fields $\vec{E} = E_0 \hat{j}$ and \vec{B} = B_0 \hat{k}. The Lorentz force is given by: $\vec{F} = q(\vec{E} + \vec{v} \times \vec{B}) = q(E_0 \hat{j} + (v_x \hat{i} + v_y \hat{j}) \times B_0 \hat{k}) = q(v_y B_0 \hat{i} + (E_0 - v_x B_0) \hat{j})$. This gives the equations of motion: $m \frac{dv_x}{dt} = q B_0 v_y$ and $m \frac{dv_y}{dt} = q(E_0 - v_x B_0)$. Defining the cyclotron frequency $\omega = \frac{q B_0}{m}$ and using $v_0 = \frac{E_0}{B_0}$, we get: $\frac{dv_x}{dt} = \omega v_y$ and $\frac{dv_y}{dt} = \omega (v_0 - v_x)$. Differentiating the second equation with respect to time gives: $\frac{d^2 v_y}{dt^2} = -\omega \frac{dv_x}{dt} = -\omega^2 v_y$. Solving this simple harmonic motion equation with the initial conditions $v_y(0) = 0$ and $v_x(0) = 3 v_0$, we find: $v_y(t) = -2 v_0 \sin(\omega t)$ and $v_x(t) = v_0 (1 + 2 \cos(\omega t))$. Integrating $v_y(t)$ to find the $y$-coordinate yields: $y(t) = \frac{2 v_0}{\omega} (\cos(\omega t) - 1)$. The minimum value of $y(t)$ occurs when $\cos(\omega t) = -1$, which implies $\sin(\omega t) = 0$. At this point, the velocity components are: $v_x = -v_0$ and $v_y = 0$, so the speed is $v = v_0$. The acceleration components at this point are: $a_x = 0$ and $a_y = \omega (v_0 - v_x) = 2 \omega v_0$. Since the velocity is purely in the $-x$ direction and the acceleration is purely in the $+y$ direction, the acceleration is entirely perpendicular to the velocity. Thus, the centripetal acceleration is $a_n = 2 \omega v_0$. The radius of curvature $R$ is: $R = \frac{v^2}{a_n} = \frac{v_0^2}{2 \omega v_0} = \frac{v_0}{2 \omega}$. Substituting $v_0 = \frac{E_0}{B_0}$ and $\omega = \frac{q B_0}{m}$, we get: $R = \frac{m E_0}{2 q B_0^2}$. This corresponds to Option A.

A block $A$ of mass $m_1 = 2\text{ kg}$ is placed on a smooth horizontal surface. Block $A$ has a vertical guide slot in which a block $C$ of mass $m_3 = 1\text{ kg}$ can slide freely without friction, meaning block $C$ can only move vertically relative to block $A$. Another block $B$ of mass $m_2 = 1\text{ kg}$ is placed on the flat horizontal top surface of block $A$. The coefficients of static and kinetic friction between block $A$ and block $B$ are $\mu_s = 0.3$ and $\mu_k = 0.2$, respectively. A light, inextensible string connects block $B$ to block $C$ over a light, frictionless pulley fixed at the top-right corner of block $A$. The system is released from rest with the horizontal segment of the string taut. Take $g = 10\text{ m/s}^2$. Which of the following statements is/are correct?

A) The acceleration of block $A$ is $\frac{8}{7}\text{ m/s}^2$ towards the left.
B) The tension in the string is $\frac{38}{7}\text{ N}$.
C) The horizontal force exerted by block $A$ on block $C$ is $\frac{8}{7}\text{ N}$.
D) The acceleration of block $B$ relative to the ground is $\frac{32}{7}\text{ m/s}^2$ towards the right.

Correct: A,B,C

Explanation:
**Insight:** By analyzing the maximum static friction between block $A$ and block $B$, we find that slipping must occur because the tension required to keep the system from slipping exceeds the maximum static friction force ($f_{s,\max} = 3\text{ N}$). Thus, kinetic friction $f_k = \mu_k m_2 g = 2\text{ N}$ acts between the blocks. We then set up the equations of motion for each block to find their accelerations and the tension in the string.

### Insight
First, we check if slipping occurs between block $A$ and block $B$. The maximum static friction force is $f_{s,\max} = \mu_s m_2 g = 0.3 \times 1 \times 10 = 3\text{ N}$. If no slipping occurred, the horizontal acceleration of the system would be zero, requiring a friction force of $10\text{ N}$ to balance the tension, which exceeds $f_{s,\max}$. Thus, slipping occurs, and kinetic friction $f_k = \mu_k m_2 g = 0.2 \times 1 \times 10 = 2\text{ N}$ acts between $A$ and $B$.

### Shared Core Derivation
Let $a_A$ be the horizontal acceleration of block $A$ towards the left. Since block $C$ is constrained to move in the vertical slot of block $A$, its horizontal acceleration is also $a_A$ towards the left.

Let $a_r$ be the acceleration of block $B$ relative to block $A$ towards the right. Since the string is inextensible, block $C$ must accelerate downwards relative to block $A$ with the same acceleration $a_r$.

Thus, the acceleration of block $B$ relative to the ground is:
$$a_B = a_r - a_A \text{ (towards the right)}$$

Now, let us write the equations of motion for each block:

1) **For block $B$ (horizontally):**
$$T - f_k = m_2 a_B \implies T - 2 = 1 \cdot (a_r - a_A) \implies T - 2 = a_r - a_A \quad \text{--- (Eq. 1)}$$

2) **For block $C$ (vertically):**
$$m_3 g - T = m_3 a_r \implies 10 - T = 1 \cdot a_r \implies T = 10 - a_r \quad \text{--- (Eq. 2)}$$

Combining Eq. 1 and Eq. 2:
$$10 - a_r - 2 = a_r - a_A \implies 2a_r - a_A = 8 \implies a_r = \frac{8 + a_A}{2} \quad \text{--- (Eq. 3)}$$

3) **For block $A$ (horizontally):**
The horizontal forces acting on block $A$ are:
- Tension $T$ towards the left (from the pulley fixed to $A$).
- Kinetic friction $f_k = 2\text{ N}$ towards the right (by Newton's third law).
- Normal force $N_{AC}$ from block $C$ towards the right.

Since block $C$ accelerates horizontally to the left with $a_A$, the normal force exerted by $A$ on $C$ is $N_{AC} = m_3 a_A$. By Newton's third law, $C$ exerts an equal and opposite force $N_{AC}$ on $A$ towards the right.

Thus, the horizontal equation of motion for block $A$ is:
$$T - f_k - N_{AC} = m_1 a_A$$
$$T - 2 - m_3 a_A = m_1 a_A \implies T - 2 = (m_1 + m_3) a_A$$

Substituting $m_1 = 2\text{ kg}$ and $m_3 = 1\text{ kg}$:
$$T - 2 = 3 a_A \quad \text{--- (Eq. 4)}$$

From Eq. 1 and Eq. 4, we get:
$$a_r - a_A = 3 a_A \implies a_r = 4 a_A$$

Substituting $a_r = 4 a_A$ into Eq. 3:
$$4 a_A = \frac{8 + a_A}{2} \implies 8 a_A = 8 + a_A \implies 7 a_A = 8 \implies a_A = \frac{8}{7}\text{ m/s}^2$$

### Judging Each Option
- **Option A**: The acceleration of block $A$ is $a_A = \frac{8}{7}\text{ m/s}^2$ towards the left. This is **TRUE**.
- **Option B**: The tension in the string is $T = 2 + 3 a_A = 2 + 3\left(\frac{8}{7}\right) = \frac{38}{7}\text{ N}$. This is **TRUE**.
- **Option C**: The horizontal force exerted by block $A$ on block $C$ is $N_{AC} = m_3 a_A = 1 \cdot \frac{8}{7} = \frac{8}{7}\text{ N}$. This is **TRUE**.
- **Option D**: The acceleration of block $B$ relative to the ground is $a_B = a_r - a_A = \frac{32}{7} - \frac{8}{7} = \frac{24}{7}\text{ m/s}^2$ towards the right. The value $\frac{32}{7}\text{ m/s}^2$ is the acceleration of $B$ relative to $A$, not relative to the ground. This is **FALSE**.

Therefore correct option(s): A,B,C

When monochromatic light of wavelength $\lambda_1 = 310\text{ nm}$ is incident on a photosensitive metallic surface, the minimum de Broglie wavelength of the emitted photoelectrons is $\lambda_{dB1}$. When the wavelength of the incident light is changed to $\lambda_2 = 620\text{ nm}$, the minimum de Broglie wavelength of the emitted photoelectrons becomes $\lambda_{dB2} = 3\lambda_{dB1}$. Now, monochromatic light of wavelength $\lambda_3 = 248\text{ nm}$ is incident on the same metallic surface, and the minimum de Broglie wavelength of the emitted photoelectrons is $\lambda_{dB3}$. When a retarding potential of $V$ volts is applied, the minimum de Broglie wavelength of the escaping photoelectrons increases to $\sqrt{13}\lambda_{dB3}$. Find the value of $V$. (Take $hc = 1240\text{ eV}\cdot\text{nm}$)

Correct: 3

Explanation:
**Insight:** The minimum de Broglie wavelength of emitted photoelectrons corresponds to the maximum kinetic energy of the photoelectrons, with the relation $K_{\text{max}} \propto \frac{1}{\lambda_{dB}^2}$. By determining the work function of the metal from the first two wavelengths, we can find the maximum kinetic energy for the third wavelength and then calculate the retarding potential required to increase the de Broglie wavelength by the given factor.

To find the retarding potential $V$, we first relate the minimum de Broglie wavelength $\lambda_{dB}$ of the photoelectrons to their maximum kinetic energy $K_{\text{max}}$ using the relation:
$$K_{\text{max}} = \frac{p^2}{2m} = \frac{h^2}{2m \lambda_{dB}^2}$$
This implies that $K_{\text{max}} \propto \frac{1}{\lambda_{dB}^2}$.

Using the Einstein's photoelectric equation, $K_{\text{max}} = \frac{hc}{\lambda} - \Phi$, where $\Phi$ is the work function of the metal. We calculate the photon energies for the first two wavelengths:
- For $\lambda_1 = 310\text{ nm}$: $E_1 = \frac{1240}{310} = 4\text{ eV}$
- For $\lambda_2 = 620\text{ nm}$: $E_2 = \frac{1240}{620} = 2\text{ eV}$

Given that $\lambda_{dB2} = 3\lambda_{dB1}$, we have:
$$\frac{K_1}{K_2} = \left(\frac{\lambda_{dB2}}{\lambda_{dB1}}\right)^2 = 9$$
Substituting the kinetic energies:
$$\frac{4 - \Phi}{2 - \Phi} = 9 \implies 4 - \Phi = 18 - 9\Phi \implies 8\Phi = 14 \implies \Phi = 1.75\text{ eV}$$

Now, for the third wavelength $\lambda_3 = 248\text{ nm}$:
- Photon energy: $E_3 = \frac{1240}{248} = 5\text{ eV}$
- Maximum kinetic energy: $K_3 = E_3 - \Phi = 5 - 1.75 = 3.25\text{ eV}$

When a retarding potential $V$ is applied, the maximum kinetic energy of the escaping photoelectrons becomes $K_3' = K_3 - eV$. The corresponding minimum de Broglie wavelength increases to $\lambda_{dB3}' = \sqrt{13}\lambda_{dB3}$.
Using the relation between kinetic energy and wavelength:
$$\frac{K_3}{K_3'} = \left(\frac{\lambda_{dB3}'}{\lambda_{dB3}}\right)^2 = 13$$
$$K_3' = \frac{K_3}{13} = \frac{3.25}{13} = 0.25\text{ eV}$$

Thus, we have:
$$K_3 - eV = 0.25 \implies 3.25 - V = 0.25 \implies V = 3\text{ V}$$

FINAL_ANSWER: 3
